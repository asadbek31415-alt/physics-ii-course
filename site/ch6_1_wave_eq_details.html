Deep Dive: The Wave Equation

Derivation from Maxwell's Equations

In a source-free vacuum ($\rho = 0, \mathbf{J} = 0$), we start with Maxwell's Equations:

$$ (i) \nabla \cdot \vec{E} = 0 \quad (ii) \nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0 $$ $$ (iii) \nabla \times \vec{E} = -\pd{\vec{B}}{t} \quad (iv) \nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \pd{\vec{E}}{t} $$

To decouple these, take the curl of Faraday's Law (iii): $$ \nabla \times (\nabla \times \vec{E}) = \nabla \times \left(-\pd{\vec{B}}{t}\right) = -\pd{}{t} (\nabla \times \vec{B}) $$

Applying the vector identity $\nabla \times (\nabla \times \vec{A}) = \nabla(\nabla \cdot \vec{A}) - \nabla^2 \vec{A}$ to the left side: $$ \nabla(\nabla \cdot \vec{E}) - \nabla^2 \vec{E} = -\pd{}{t} \left( \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \pd{\vec{E}}{t} \right) $$

Since $\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = 0$ in vacuum, the first term vanishes, leaving:

$$ \nabla^2 \vec{E} = \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial^2 \vec{E}}{\partial t^2} $$

This matches the standard 3D wave equation $\nabla^2 f = \frac{1}{v^2}\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial t^2}$ where the speed is $c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0} \approx 3 \times 10^8$ m/s.

The Transverse Nature

Consider a monochromatic plane wave propagating in the $z$-direction: $$ \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(z, t) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0 e^{i(kz - \omega t)}, \quad \tilde{\mathbf{B}}(z, t) = \tilde{\mathbf{B}}_0 e^{i(kz - \omega t)} $$ Applying $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = 0$ (Gauss's Law): $$ \frac{\partial E_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial E_y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial E_z}{\partial z} = 0 \implies i k \tilde{E}_{0z} e^{i(kz-\omega t)} = 0 \implies \tilde{E}_{0z} = 0 $$

This proves that electromagnetic waves are transverse: the field oscillates perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

Phase and Amplitude Relationships

Faraday's Law ($\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\pd{\mathbf{B}}{t}$) imposes a strict relationship between the magnitudes and phases of the fields. For a wave in the $+\hat{z}$ direction: $$ \nabla \times \mathbf{E} = \left| \begin{matrix} \hat{x} & \hat{y} & \hat{z} \\ 0 & 0 & \pd{}{z} \\ E_x & E_y & 0 \end{matrix} \right| = -\pd{E_y}{z}\hat{x} + \pd{E_x}{z}\hat{y} $$ Substituting the exponential forms: $$ -ik\tilde{E}_{0y}\hat{x} + ik\tilde{E}_{0x}\hat{y} = -(-i\omega)\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_0 \implies \tilde{\mathbf{B}}_0 = \frac{k}{\omega} (\hat{z} \times \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0) $$

Core Relationships

$\vec{E} \perp \vec{B}$ Perpendicularity. The electric and magnetic fields are mutually perpendicular AND both are perpendicular to the direction of travel ($\hat{k}$).
In Phase Both fields reach their peaks and zeros at identical locations and times.
$E_0 = c B_0$ Amplitude Ratio. The magnitude of the electric field is always $c$ times larger than that of the magnetic field.
