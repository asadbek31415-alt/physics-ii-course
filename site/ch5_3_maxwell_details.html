Deep Dive: The Equations of Light

Maxwell's equations summarize all of classical electromagnetism in four elegant lines. They predict that electric and magnetic fields can propagate through space as waves—moving at the speed of light.

The Four Equations (In Vacuum)

$$ \begin{aligned} \nabla \cdot \vec{E} &= \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0} & \quad \text{(Gauss's Law)} \\ \nabla \cdot \vec{B} &= 0 & \quad \text{(No Magnetic Monopoles)} \\ \nabla \times \vec{E} &= -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t} & \quad \text{(Faraday's Law)} \\ \nabla \times \vec{B} &= \mu_0 \vec{J} + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t} & \quad \text{(Ampère-Maxwell Law)} \end{aligned} $$

Symmetry and Duality

In empty space (where $\rho = 0$ and $\vec{J} = 0$), the equations take on a beautiful symmetry.

$$ \begin{aligned} \nabla \cdot \vec{E} &= 0 \\ \nabla \cdot \vec{B} &= 0 \\ \nabla \times \vec{E} &= -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t} \\ \nabla \times \vec{B} &= \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t} \end{aligned} $$

If you swap $\vec{E} \rightarrow \vec{B}$ and $\vec{B} \rightarrow -\mu_0 \epsilon_0 \vec{E}$, the equations remain structurally the same! This symmetry hints at the deep connection between electricity, magnetism, and light.
