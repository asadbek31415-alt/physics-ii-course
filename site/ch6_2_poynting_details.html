Deep Dive: The Poynting Vector

Poynting's Theorem: Derivation

How much work does an EM field do on a collection of charges? The rate of work per unit volume is given by the power density: $$ \vec{f} \cdot \vec{v} = \rho(\vec{E} + \vec{v}\times\vec{B}) \cdot \vec{v} = \vec{J}\cdot\vec{E} $$

From Ampère's Law (with Maxwell's correction), we solve for current density $\vec{J}$: $$ \vec{J} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\curl\vec{B} - \epsilon_0\pd{\vec{E}}{t} $$

Substituting this into the power equation:

$$ \vec{J}\cdot\vec{E} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(\curl\vec{B})\cdot\vec{E} - \epsilon_0\vec{E}\cdot\pd{\vec{E}}{t} $$

Using the vector product rule $\div(\vec{E}\times\vec{B}) = \vec{B}\cdot(\curl\vec{E}) - \vec{E}\cdot(\curl\vec{B})$ and Faraday's Law ($\curl\vec{E} = -\pd{\vec{B}}{t}$):

$$ \vec{J}\cdot\vec{E} = -\frac{1}{\mu_0}\div(\vec{E}\times\vec{B}) - \frac{1}{\mu_0}\vec{B}\cdot\pd{\vec{B}}{t} - \epsilon_0\vec{E}\cdot\pd{\vec{E}}{t} $$

Recognizing that $\vec{A}\cdot\pd{\vec{A}}{t} = \frac{1}{2}\pd{A^2}{t}$, we obtain the integral form of Poynting's Theorem:

$$ \frac{dW}{dt} = -\frac{d}{dt} \int_V \frac{1}{2}\left( \epsilon_0 E^2 + \frac{1}{\mu_0} B^2 \right) d\tau - \oint_S \vec{S} \cdot d\vec{a} $$

Conservation of Energy: The work done per unit time on the charges ($dW/dt$) equals the rate at which field energy decreases minus the rate at which energy flows out through the bounding surface.

Intensity

For sinusoidal waves, the rapid fluctuations of $\mathbf{S}$ are usually averaged out. The average power per unit area, called intensity ($I$), is:

$$ I = \langle S \rangle = \frac{1}{2} c \epsilon_0 E_0^2 $$
