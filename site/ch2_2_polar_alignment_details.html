Deep Dive: Alignment of Polar Molecules

Unlike neutral atoms which must be stretched to create a dipole, polar molecules (like water) have a permanent built-in dipole moment \(\vec{p}\). When placed in an electric field, the physics is governed by Torque and Temperature.

1. Torque on a Dipole

Consider a dipole consisting of a charge \(+q\) and \(-q\) separated by a vector \(\vec{d}\). In a uniform electric field \(\vec{E}\):

  • The force on \(+q\) is \(\vec{F}_+ = q\vec{E}\).
  • The force on \(-q\) is \(\vec{F}_- = -q\vec{E}\).

The net force is zero, so the molecule doesn't accelerate linearly. However, because the forces act at different points, they create a Torque or Mechanical Moment (\(\mathbf{M}\)) that rotates the molecule.

$$ \mathbf{M} = (\vec{r}_+ \times \vec{F}_+) + (\vec{r}_- \times \vec{F}_-) = \vec{p} \times \vec{E} $$

This torque acts to align the dipole moment \(\vec{p}\) parallel to the field \(\vec{E}\).

2. Potential Energy

Rotation requires work. The work done to rotate the dipole against the electric field is stored as potential energy \(U\).

$$ U = - \vec{p} \cdot \vec{E} = -pE \cos \theta $$

where \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\vec{p}\) and \(\vec{E}\).

  • Minimum Energy (\(\theta = 0\)): Aligned with field. Stable equilibrium.
  • Maximum Energy (\(\theta = \pi\)): Anti-aligned. Unstable equilibrium.

3. The Battle: Field vs. Heat

If torque were the only factor, every molecule in a dielectric would line up perfectly with the field, creating massive polarization. In reality, this doesn't happen. Why?

Thermal Agitation.

At any temperature \(T > 0\), molecules are randomly colliding and rotating. This thermal energy (\(k_B T\)) tends to randomize the orientation of the dipoles.

The Balance

The degree of alignment depends on the ratio of electric potential energy to thermal energy: $$ \frac{pE}{k_B T} $$ At room temperature, for typical fields, this ratio is very small (\(\ll 1\)). This means the alignment is actually very slight—only a small fraction of molecules succeed in pointing along the field at any moment.

However, because there are Avogadro's number of molecules, even a tiny fractional alignment results in a significant macroscopic Polarization \(\vec{P}\).
