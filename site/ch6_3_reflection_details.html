Deep Dive: Boundary Conditions & Reflection

The Four Boundary Conditions

To determine what happens at a boundary between two media, we apply Maxwell's equations in integral form to a tiny "pillbox" or a thin loop at the interface. This gives us the general boundary conditions:

$$ \begin{align} 1. \quad & \epsilon_1 E_{1\perp} - \epsilon_2 E_{2\perp} = \sigma_f \\ 2. \quad & B_{1\perp} - B_{2\perp} = 0 \\ 3. \quad & \vec{E}_{1\parallel} - \vec{E}_{2\parallel} = 0 \\ 4. \quad & \frac{1}{\mu_1} \vec{B}_{1\parallel} - \frac{1}{\mu_2} \vec{B}_{2\parallel} = \vec{K}_f \times \hat{n} \end{align} $$

For linear, non-conducting media ($\sigma_f=0, K_f=0$), the tangential components of $\vec{E}$ and $\vec{H}$ must be continuous.

Derivation for Normal Incidence

Suppose a wave polarized in the $x$-direction strikes a boundary (the $xy$-plane at $z=0$). The incident, reflected, and transmitted waves are: $$ \tilde{\vec{E}}_I = \tilde{E}_{0I} e^{i(k_1 z - \omega t)}\hat{x}, \quad \tilde{\vec{E}}_R = \tilde{E}_{0R} e^{i(-k_1 z - \omega t)}\hat{x}, \quad \tilde{\vec{E}}_T = \tilde{E}_{0T} e^{i(k_2 z - \omega t)}\hat{x} $$ From the continuity of $\vec{E}_{\parallel}$ at $z=0$: $$ \tilde{E}_{0I} + \tilde{E}_{0R} = \tilde{E}_{0T} $$ From the continuity of $\vec{H}_{\parallel}$ (assuming $\mu_1 \approx \mu_2 \approx \mu_0$): $$ \frac{1}{v_1} \tilde{E}_{0I} - \frac{1}{v_1} \tilde{E}_{0R} = \frac{1}{v_2} \tilde{E}_{0T} \implies n_1 (\tilde{E}_{0I} - \tilde{E}_{0R}) = n_2 \tilde{E}_{0T} $$

Solving this system of two equations yields the Fresnel Equations for normal incidence:

$$ \tilde{E}_{0R} = \left( \frac{n_1 - n_2}{n_1 + n_2} \right) \tilde{E}_{0I}, \quad \tilde{E}_{0T} = \left( \frac{2n_1}{n_1 + n_2} \right) \tilde{E}_{0I} $$

The Phase Shift Mystery

Looking at the reflection coefficient $r = \frac{n_1 - n_2}{n_1 + n_2}$, we see something interesting:

$n_1 > n_2$ In Phase. Dense to rare transition. $r$ is positive.
$n_2 > n_1$ Phase Shift. Rare to dense transition. Reflected wave undergoes a $180^\circ$ phase shift.

This is analogous to a transverse wave on a string hitting a fixed end (phase shift) vs. a free end (no shift).
