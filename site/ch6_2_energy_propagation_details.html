Deep Dive: Energy and Momentum

The Poynting Vector

Electromagnetic waves carry energy. The energy flux density (energy per unit area, per unit time) is given by the Poynting vector, $\mathbf{S}$:

$$ \mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{\mu_0} (\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B}) $$

For a monochromatic plane wave traveling in vacuum, $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are in phase and perpendicular. The magnitude of the Poynting vector fluctuates rapidly at frequency $2\omega$. Usually, we are interested in the intensity $I$, which is the time-averaged magnitude of $\mathbf{S}$:

$$ I = \langle S \rangle = \frac{1}{2} c \epsilon_0 E_0^2 $$

Momentum and Radiation Pressure

Electromagnetic waves also carry momentum. The momentum density $\mathbf{g}$ stored in the fields is:

$$ \mathbf{g} = \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{c^2} \mathbf{S} $$

When an electromagnetic wave strikes a surface, it transfers momentum, resulting in a force. This is the origin of radiation pressure ($P$).

Absorber For a black body surface: \(P = I/c\).
Reflector For a perfect mirror: \(P = 2I/c\).
