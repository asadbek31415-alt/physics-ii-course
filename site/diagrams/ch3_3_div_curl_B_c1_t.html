The Ideal Solenoid

An ideal solenoid is infinitely long with tightly wound turns. The magnetic field behaves simply:

Inside

The field is Uniform and parallel to the axis.

$$ B = \mu_0 n I $$

Outside

In the ideal approximation, the field is Zero everywhere outside the coil.

Ampère's Law

1. Loop Choice

We choose a Rectangular Loop (Yellow) of length $L$.

2. The Integral

  • Side 1 (Inside): Contribution = $BL$.
  • Sides 2 & 4: Contribution = $0$.
  • Side 3 (Outside): Contribution = $0$.
$$ \oint \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = BL $$

3. Solve

$$ BL = \mu_0 (n L I) \implies B = \mu_0 n I $$

Why is B outside Zero?

The Outside Loop

Consider a second rectangular loop (Green) placed entirely outside.

Evaluation

This loop encloses NO Current ($I_{\text{enc}} = 0$).

$$ \oint \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = [B(a) - B(b)]L = 0 $$

This implies $B(a) = B(b)$. The field is constant everywhere outside.

Conclusion

Since the field must vanish at infinity, it must be Zero everywhere outside.
