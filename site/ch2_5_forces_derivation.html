Deep Dive: Force on a Dielectric Slab

Calculating the force on a dielectric slab using the microscopic forces on individual dipoles in a fringing field is extremely difficult. Instead, we use the Principle of Virtual Work (Energy Method), which ignores the details of the field and focuses on the total energy of the system.

1. The Energy Argument

Consider a parallel-plate capacitor with width \(w\), length \(l\), and separation \(d\). A dielectric slab is inserted a distance \(x\).

If the system is connected to a battery (constant voltage \(V\)), the battery does work as the slab moves. The net force is given by:

$$ F = + \frac{1}{2} V^2 \frac{dC}{dx} $$

2. Modeling Capacitance

We can treat the setup as two capacitors in parallel: one filled with dielectric (length \(x\)) and one filled with air (length \(l-x\)).

$$ C_{diel} = \frac{\epsilon_0 \epsilon_r w x}{d} \quad \text{and} \quad C_{air} = \frac{\epsilon_0 w (l-x)}{d} $$

The total capacitance is the sum:

$$ C(x) = \frac{\epsilon_0 w}{d} [ \epsilon_r x + l - x ] = \frac{\epsilon_0 w}{d} [ l + x(\epsilon_r - 1) ] $$

Recall that \(\chi_e = \epsilon_r - 1\). Thus:

$$ C(x) = \frac{\epsilon_0 w}{d} (l + \chi_e x) $$

3. Calculating the Force

Now we differentiate the capacitance with respect to \(x\):

$$ \frac{dC}{dx} = \frac{\epsilon_0 w \chi_e}{d} $$

Substituting this into the force equation for constant voltage (\(F = \frac{1}{2}V^2 \frac{dC}{dx}\)):

$$ F = \frac{\epsilon_0 \chi_e w}{2d} V^2 $$

Interpretation

Since \(\chi_e\) is positive for all dielectrics, the force \(F\) is positive (in the direction of increasing \(x\)).

This confirms that the dielectric is always pulled into the capacitor. The force depends on the square of the voltage, so it pulls regardless of the polarity of the plates.
