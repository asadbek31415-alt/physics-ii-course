Deep Dive: Magnetic Field of a Straight Wire

Problem: Find the magnetic field a distance $s$ from a long straight wire carrying a steady current $I$.

1. Setup the Biot-Savart Law

We begin with the general formula. The wire is aligned along the z-axis. The direction of the field, determined by the cross product $d\mathbf{l}' \times \mathbf{\hat{r}}$, is circumferential (into the page at point P). Thus, we only need to integrate the magnitude.

$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \int \frac{d\mathbf{l}' \times \mathbf{\hat{r}}}{r^2} $$

The magnitude of the cross product is $|d\mathbf{l}'| \sin(\alpha)$, where $\alpha$ is the angle between the current and the displacement vector. Looking at the geometry, if we define the angle $\theta$ from the horizontal $s$, then $\alpha = 90^\circ + \theta$, and $\sin(90^\circ+\theta) = \cos(\theta)$.

$$ dB = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \frac{dz \cos\theta}{r^2} $$

2. Trigonometric Substitution

We have three variables changing as we integrate along the wire: $z$, $r$, and $\theta$. It is easiest to express everything in terms of the angle $\theta$.

From the triangle in the diagram:

1. Relate distance $r$ to $s$: $$ r = \frac{s}{\cos\theta} $$

2. Relate height $z$ to $s$: $$ z = s \tan\theta $$

3. Differentiate $z$ to get $dz$: $$ dz = s \sec^2\theta \, d\theta = \frac{s}{\cos^2\theta} d\theta $$

3. Solving the Integral

Substitute these terms back into the Biot-Savart expression:

$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \int \frac{\left( \frac{s}{\cos^2\theta} d\theta \right) \cos\theta}{\left( \frac{s}{\cos\theta} \right)^2} $$

Simplifying the fraction:

$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \int \frac{s \cos\theta}{s^2} d\theta = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi s} \int \cos\theta \, d\theta $$

For an infinite wire, the angle $\theta$ spans from $-\pi/2$ (bottom infinity) to $+\pi/2$ (top infinity).

$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi s} \left[ \sin\theta \right]_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi s} (1 - (-1)) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi s} (2) $$

Final Result

The magnetic field at distance $s$ from an infinite straight wire is:

$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi s} $$

The direction follows the Right-Hand Rule: if your thumb points along the current $I$, your fingers curl in the direction of $\mathbf{B}$.
