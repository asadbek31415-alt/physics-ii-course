Deep Dive: Capacitance & Energy

Deriving the Parallel Plate formula and calculating Energy Storage.

1. The Parallel Plate Capacitor

The most common capacitor geometry consists of two large parallel metal plates of area $A$, separated by a small distance $d$. Let's calculate its capacitance $C = Q/V$ from first principles.

  • Find the Electric Field ($E$)

    From Gauss's Law, the field near a charged plane is $\sigma / 2\epsilon_0$. Since we have two plates (one $+Q$, one $-Q$), their fields add up in the middle:

    $$ E = \frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0} + \frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0} = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{A\epsilon_0} $$
  • Find the Potential Difference ($V$)

    The potential difference is the work per unit charge to move from one plate to the other. Since the field is uniform:

    $$ V = \int \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{l} = E \cdot d = \left( \frac{Q}{A\epsilon_0} \right) d $$
  • Calculate Capacitance ($C$)

    Using the definition $C \equiv Q/V$:

    $$ C = \frac{Q}{ \left( \frac{Qd}{A\epsilon_0} \right) } = \frac{\epsilon_0 A}{d} $$

2. Energy Storage

To charge a capacitor, you must remove electrons from the positive plate and force them onto the negative plate. The first electron is free, but subsequent electrons face repulsion. You have to do Work against the Electric Field.

At any instant where the charge is $q$, the potential difference is $v = q/C$. The work $dW$ to move an additional slice of charge $dq$ is:

$$ dW = v \cdot dq = \frac{q}{C} dq $$

Integrating from $0$ to total charge $Q$:

$$ W = \int_{0}^{Q} \frac{q}{C} dq = \frac{1}{C} \left[ \frac{q^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{Q} = \frac{Q^2}{2C} $$

Since $Q=CV$, we can rewrite this in the most famous form:

$$ W = \frac{1}{2} C V^2 $$

This work is stored as potential energy in the electric field between the plates.
