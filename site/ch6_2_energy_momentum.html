Energy and Momentum

1. The Poynting Vector

Electromagnetic waves transport energy. The energy per unit time, per unit area, transported by the fields is given by the Poynting vector, $\mathbf{S}$:

$$ \mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{\mu_0} (\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B}) $$

As waves propagate through media (conductors), they lose energy, and their amplitude decays. This is often characterized by the "skin depth".

2. Radiation Pressure

Electromagnetic waves also carry momentum. When a wave strikes a surface, it transfers this momentum, exerting a force known as radiation pressure.
For complete absorption, pressure $P = I/c$. For perfect reflection, $P = 2I/c$.
