Deep Dive: Gauge Freedom

The Non-Uniqueness of A

Because the magnetic field is defined as a curl ($\mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}$), the vector potential $\mathbf{A}$ is not unique. We can add the gradient of any scalar function $\lambda$ to $\mathbf{A}$ without changing the physical physics.

\[ \mathbf{A}' = \mathbf{A} + \nabla \lambda \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \nabla \times \mathbf{A}' = \nabla \times \mathbf{A} = \mathbf{B} \]

Why does this happen?

The slider adds a "radial" component (pointing straight out from the center). Radial fields have zero curl (they don't rotate).

Even though the arrows on the left look drastically different—changing from a circular flow to an outward explosion—the amount of "net rotation" at any specific point remains exactly the same. Therefore, $\mathbf{B}$ stays constant.
