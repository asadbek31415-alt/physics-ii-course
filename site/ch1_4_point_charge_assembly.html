Deep Dive: Point Charge Assembly

How much work does it take to assemble an entire collection of point charges? We imagine bringing the charges in, one by one, from "far away" (infinity), where the potential is zero.

Step-by-Step Construction

Charge 1 (\(q_1\)):

Bringing in the first charge takes no work (\(W_1 = 0\)), since there is no field yet to fight against.

Charge 2 (\(q_2\)):

Now bring in \(q_2\). It feels the potential created by \(q_1\). The work required is:

$$ W_2 = q_2 V_1(\vec{r}_2) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} q_2 \left( \frac{q_1}{r_{12}} \right) $$
Charge 3 (\(q_3\)):

Now bring in \(q_3\); this requires work against the combined potential of \(q_1\) and \(q_2\):

$$ W_3 = q_3 [V_1(\vec{r}_3) + V_2(\vec{r}_3)] = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} q_3 \left( \frac{q_1}{r_{13}} + \frac{q_2}{r_{23}} \right) $$
Charge 4 (\(q_4\)):
$$ W_4 = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} q_4 \left( \frac{q_1}{r_{14}} + \frac{q_2}{r_{24}} + \frac{q_3}{r_{34}} \right) $$

The General Rule

The total work necessary to assemble the first $n$ charges is the sum of these individual steps. The pattern is clear: take the product of each pair of charges, divide by their separation distance, and add it all up.

$$ W = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j > i}^n \frac{q_i q_j}{r_{ij}} $$

The stipulation \(j > i\) is to remind you not to count the same pair twice (e.g., once as \(q_1 q_2\) and again as \(q_2 q_1\)).

Counting Double

A "nicer" way to accomplish this is intentionally to count each pair twice, and then divide by 2:

$$ W = \frac{1}{8\pi\epsilon_0} \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j \neq i}^n \frac{q_i q_j}{r_{ij}} $$

In this form, the answer plainly does not depend on the order in which you assemble the charges. Finally, we can pull out the factor \(q_i\):

$$ W = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^n q_i \left( \sum_{j \neq i}^n \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{q_j}{r_{ij}} \right) $$

The term in the parentheses is the potential at point \(\vec{r}_i\) (the position of \(q_i\)) due to all the other charges. Thus:

$$ W = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^n q_i V(\vec{r}_i) $$
