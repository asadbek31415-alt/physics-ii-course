Deep Dive: Magnetic Monopoles & Symmetry

1. The Missing Terms

In a vacuum where $\rho$ and $\vec{J}$ are zero, Maxwell's equations are perfectly symmetric. However, in the presence of sources, they look lopsided.

Electric fields have point sources (electric charges, $\rho_e$). Magnetic fields do not; magnetic field lines always close on themselves ($\div \vec{B} = 0$). This lack of "Magnetic Charge" (Monopoles) breaks the aesthetic symmetry of the universe.

2. If Monopoles Existed...

If nature allowed for magnetic charge density ($\rho_m$) and magnetic current density ($\vec{J}_m$), Maxwell's equations would become perfectly symmetrical pairs.

Standard World
$$ \begin{align} \div \vec{E} &= \frac{1}{\epsilon_0}\rho_e \\ \div \vec{B} &= 0 \\ \curl \vec{E} &= -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t} \\ \curl \vec{B} &= \mu_0 \vec{J}_e + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t} \end{align} $$
Symmetrical World
$$ \begin{align} \div \vec{E} &= \frac{1}{\epsilon_0}\rho_e \\ \div \vec{B} &= \mu_0 \class{added-term}{\rho_m} \\ \curl \vec{E} &= -\class{added-term}{\mu_0 \vec{J}_m} -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t} \\ \curl \vec{B} &= \mu_0 \vec{J}_e + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t} \end{align} $$

In this theoretical framework:

  • $\rho_m$ represents "North" (+) or "South" (-) magnetic charges.
  • $\vec{J}_m$ represents the flow of these magnetic charges.
  • The term $-\mu_0 \vec{J}_m$ in Faraday's law would mean a flow of magnetic monopoles generates an electric field directly.

3. The Duality Transformation

Maxwell's equations exhibit a property called Duality. If you rotate the fields and sources by an angle $\alpha$:

$$ \begin{aligned} \vec{E}' &= \vec{E} \cos \alpha + c \vec{B} \sin \alpha \\ c \vec{B}' &= c \vec{B} \cos \alpha - \vec{E} \sin \alpha \end{aligned} $$

...and transform charges ($\rho_e, \rho_m$) similarly, the form of Maxwell's equations remains unchanged.

Implication: We could technically define all particles as having a mix of electric and magnetic charge. By convention, we chose $\alpha$ such that all magnetic charge is zero ($\rho_m = 0$), dumping all the "charge" into the electric term $\rho_e$.

4. Why do we care?

Even though we have never observed a magnetic monopole, they play a crucial role in theoretical physics.

Dirac's Quantization Condition: Paul Dirac showed in 1931 that if even a single magnetic monopole exists anywhere in the universe, quantum mechanics requires that electric charge must be quantized (which it is—charge comes in discrete packets of $e$).

This would explain why every electron has exactly the same charge, one of the greatest mysteries in physics.
