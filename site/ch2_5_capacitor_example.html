Deep Dive: Capacitor with Dielectric

Problem: A parallel plate capacitor with plate area \(A\) and separation \(d\) is filled with a linear dielectric material of permittivity \(\epsilon\). Calculate the capacitance.

The Strategy: Use D First

If we tried to calculate the Electric Field \(\vec{E}\) directly, we would struggle because we don't know the bound charges on the dielectric surface yet.

However, we know the Free Charge on the metal plates (\(+Q\) and \(-Q\)). This makes \(\vec{D}\) the perfect tool.

Step 1: Find the Displacement (D)

Using Gauss's Law for free charge \(\oint \vec{D} \cdot d\vec{a} = Q_{f,enc}\), we draw a Gaussian pillbox around the top plate. The free charge density is \(\sigma_f = Q/A\).

$$ D = \sigma_f = \frac{Q}{A} $$

(Pointing downwards, from + to -)

Step 2: Find the Electric Field (E)

Now we use the property of the linear dielectric: \(\vec{D} = \epsilon \vec{E}\).

$$ E = \frac{D}{\epsilon} = \frac{Q}{\epsilon A} $$

Note: If this were vacuum, we would use \(\epsilon_0\). Since \(\epsilon > \epsilon_0\), the electric field is reduced inside the material.

Step 3: Find the Potential (V)

The potential difference is the integral of the field over the distance \(d\):

$$ V = E d = \frac{Q d}{\epsilon A} $$

Step 4: Calculate Capacitance (C)

By definition, \(C = Q/V\):

$$ C = \frac{\epsilon A}{d} = \epsilon_r \frac{\epsilon_0 A}{d} = \epsilon_r C_{vac} $$

Physical Intuition: Shielding

Why does the capacitance increase?

The dielectric material gets polarized. Positive ends of the dipoles point toward the negative plate, and negative ends point toward the positive plate.

This creates a Bound Surface Charge that opposes the free charge on the plates. This "shielding" effect cancels out some of the Electric Field.

  • Since \(E\) is weaker, the voltage \(V = Ed\) is lower for the same amount of charge \(Q\).
  • Since \(C = Q/V\), a lower \(V\) (denominator) means a higher Capacitance.

Conclusion

For a parallel-plate capacitor filled with one linear dielectric material, the vacuum result

$$ C_{vac} = \frac{\epsilon_0 A}{d} $$

becomes

$$ C = \frac{\epsilon A}{d} = \epsilon_r C_{vac} $$

The constant \(\epsilon_0\) itself has not changed. It is the permittivity of empty space. When the space between the plates is filled with a dielectric, the material responds to the field, so the relevant permittivity becomes \(\epsilon = \epsilon_r \epsilon_0\).

This is the same factor that appears in the capacitance. In vacuum, a certain free charge produces a certain field. In the dielectric, bound charges partly oppose that field, so the same free charge produces a smaller \(E\) and a smaller voltage \(V = Ed\). Since \(C = Q/V\), the capacitance is larger by the factor \(\epsilon_r\).

A useful shortcut is: when a region that used to be vacuum is completely filled with a uniform linear dielectric, many vacuum formulas that contain \(\epsilon_0\) become the material formulas by replacing \(\epsilon_0\) with \(\epsilon\). The replacement is not magic; it is a compact way to include the material's polarization response.
