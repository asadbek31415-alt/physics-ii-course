Deep Dive: The DISPLACEMENT CURRENT

Maxwell fixed Ampère's Law by adding a new term, $\mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t}$, which acts like a current. We call this the Displacement Current.

The Capacitor Paradox

Imagine calculating the magnetic field around a wire charging a capacitor. Using Ampère's Law $\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}$, we define an Amperian loop around the wire.

  • Surface 1: A flat disk cutting through the wire. Current $I$ passes through it. $B \neq 0$.
  • Surface 2: A "balloon" shape that passes between the capacitor plates. No real current passes through this surface! So $I_{\text{enc}} = 0$, implying $B=0$.

This ambiguity is resolved by the displacement current. Between the plates, $\vec{E}$ is changing. The integration of the displacement current density $\vec{J}_d = \epsilon_0 \dot{\vec{E}}$ over Surface 2 gives exactly $I$, matching the conduction current in Surface 1.

The Equation

The full form of Ampère's Law (with Maxwell's correction) is:

$$ \nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0 \vec{J} + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t} $$
