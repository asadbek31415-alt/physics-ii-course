Deep Dive: The Flaw in Ampère's Law

Before Maxwell, Ampère's Law was written as $\nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0 \vec{J}$. Maxwell noticed a fatal mathematical contradiction in this equation for non-steady currents.

The Mathematical Contradiction

Take the divergence of the curl of any vector field. It must be zero (vector identity):

$$ \nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{B}) = 0 $$

However, taking the divergence of the right side of Ampère's Law gives:

$$ \nabla \cdot (\mu_0 \vec{J}) = \mu_0 (\nabla \cdot \vec{J}) $$

From the continuity equation (conservation of charge), $\nabla \cdot \vec{J} = -\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t}$. This is only zero for steady currents ($\dot{\rho} = 0$). If charge is piling up (like on a capacitor plate), the equation breaks!

$$ 0 \neq -\mu_0 \dot{\rho} \quad \text{(Contradiction!)} $$
